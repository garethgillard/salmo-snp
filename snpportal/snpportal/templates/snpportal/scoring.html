How a score is made

A SNP's score adds up the weights of the kinds of evidence it has, counted over all {{ conditions }} conditions (tissues, maturity and sex, immune stimulation, embryo stages). Each kind counts once: chromatin counts its best state over the conditions, active or else weak. Scores are coloured in bands,{% for t, b in bands.items %} {{ b }}{% endfor %}, and 0 means no evidence was found, not evidence of no effect.

Layers with weight 0 are shown and never counted: molQTL lead SNPs, sequence that aligns to pike, ATAC summits and the ChromBPNet allele effects. Each waits for a test of what it adds, or for the maintainer's decision.

You can set your own weights and band cut-offs, on the Score page or with Weights in a result's Table. The whole result is scored again, and every page, report and download says which rules made it. The rules themselves are on Methods.

Does the score work?

  • It carries signal, modestly. eQTL lead SNPs score 3 or more 1.56 (1.32–1.83) times as often as matched SNPs (1.47 in design T), and the GWAS leads 1.51 (0.95–2.33) times. Known loci: 5.2 (2.9–9.3), from few loci, a quarter of them coding. caQTL leads: 3.2 (2.8–3.6), circular.
  • There is no gradient. Scores of 1–2 are as enriched as 3–4 (1.60 against 1.64), and score by score the odds stay near 1.7. A plain "any evidence, yes or no" ranks the eQTL leads as well as the score does (AUC 0.539, 0.528–0.550).
  • It does not beat distance. Distance to the nearest protein-coding gene start ranks eQTL leads a little better (AUC 0.562). That is the eQTL bias the literature describes: GWAS hits, unlike eQTL, do not cluster at gene starts (Mostafavi 2023), so distance is a control here, not evidence.
  • The open choices do not change it. No option for the active and weak states or the synonymous weight moves the eQTL result measurably (odds ratios 1.44 to 1.58, AUC within 0.004), though they move many SNPs.
  • The GWAS signal is coding, not chromatin. The maturity GWAS's leads are enriched in coding sequence (2.65) and not in active states (0.81), but its leads are array tags, which favour coding SNPs.
  • The GWAS prior is too steep. The Association page's fine-mapping prior, exp({{ lambda|default:0.5 }} × score), gives 4.5-fold prior odds at score 3 and 12-fold at 5. The maturity GWAS supports a slope of 0.05 to 0.15 per point, and 0.5 fits worse than a flat prior. Inside its loci the posterior does not sit on high-scoring SNPs.

What the tests cannot say yet: the right size of any weight, a choice between the options, or whether chromatin evidence should be graded. That needs a better truth set: allelic imbalance in the 12 fish, the ChromBPNet effects, a large fine-mapped eQTL study, or a company's own GWAS.

Reading a score

  • A score says a SNP lies in a region with functional evidence. It does not say the SNP is causal, and SNPs in the same element tie.
  • For a GWAS locus, compare scores among the SNPs linked to the lead SNP.
  • For a chip, use the score as a preference inside genome bins, after reliability, spacing and allele frequency, as the panel designer does. Combined with your own GWAS, this follows the one design with a documented gain (XT-50K, in cattle).
  • For genomic prediction within one population, expect little: in the studies reviewed the gain was small or nil. Estimate any gain in fish that took no part in choosing the markers.

What other projects do

Nobody has a proven set of weights to copy. The transparent human schemes were validated only by enrichment; the learned human scores work for rare, damaging variants and do hardly better than chance for common variants inside one LD block; the livestock scores that work were learned from trait records of 44,000 to 103,000 cattle. No aquaculture species has a trained or validated score.

So the score keeps a basic, transparent additive form, the form of both published livestock scores and of the standard fine-mapping priors; the order of the evidence comes from the literature, and its scale should come from our own tests. The literature orders the evidence differently from the default weights in three places: a protein change and evolutionary constraint carry more per SNP than a regulatory element; an active promoter more than an active enhancer; and a synonymous change is not a protein change. These are open decisions (below).

What orders SNPs inside one locus is evidence at the base, not the region: a predicted allele effect, allelic imbalance, a protein change. Chromatin states separate regions from the background, and then tie.
